Today's options

Every option today has a catch.

Waiting

A good medical interpreter is rarely there when the ED needs one.

Harm

Up to 1 in 12 machine-translated discharge sentences could cause significant harm.

Privacy

In a small community, the interpreter may know the patient.

No record

Consumer apps leave no audit trail and no safety testing.

Sources: Khoong et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2019 (8% of Chinese, 2% of Spanish sentences); Cronin et al., BJGP Open 2025; Challenge 6 brief.

Speaker: Chijindu Okafor (1:00 to 1:15). "Every option today has a catch. Interpreters are excellent but rarely there in the moment. Consumer apps like Google Translate: in one emergency-department study, up to one in twelve discharge sentences could have caused significant harm, and a 2025 review found no evidence they've been safety-tested for live consultations. In a community this size, the interpreter might be the patient's neighbour. And nothing is recorded."
