Testing in the hospital

Each language earns a licence first.

STEP 1

Bench

TUH phrases in every accent, over recorded ED noise. Interpreters rate each error.

STEP 2

Simulation

Role-play with native-speaker actors and TUH clinicians.

STEP 3

Shadow

Runs beside a real interpreter, who scores every line. No patient relies on it.

STEP 4

Pilot

One outpatient clinic, routine and clinical tiers only, 100% audited.

STEP 5

Scale

Wards, then the ED. 5% random interpreter audit, for ever.

Speaker: Bakht Zia Khan (3:55 to 4:20). "Testing is how clinicians come to trust it. Each language earns a licence, step by step: bench testing with TUH's own phrases and accents over real ED noise; simulation with actors; shadow mode beside a real interpreter, where no patient relies on it; then a supervised pilot in one outpatient clinic, where most interpreter bookings happen anyway; then scale. Last night we stress-tested the safety net: we planted 28 dangerous errors into real translations, and it caught 27, with zero false alarms. The first run caught 26; it found a weakness with sentences like 'do not stop warfarin without asking', and we fixed it within the hour. That's exactly what this testing is for." Languages earn their licence for real: overnight we tested all 37 languages on 20 medical sentences; 28 passed and 9 were removed. Emergency mode only offers languages that also have speech recognition and a voice. If asked about the one miss: a half-deleted 'not' ('eat and don't drink anything') that the back-translation smoothed over. It's on the fix list, and it's why clinical sentences still need the review step.
